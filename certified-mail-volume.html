Certified Mail volume, year over year

Every figure on this page comes from the USPS Revenue, Pieces, and Weight reports. We publish it because the trend is the clearest argument for why accountable mail deserves its own representation, and because nobody else is tracking it in one place.

The headline

In fiscal year 2025, Certified Mail volume grew 2.1 percent to 159.8 million pieces. Over the same period, total market dominant mail fell 3.2 percent and First-Class Mail fell 5.1 percent.

That was the second consecutive year of Certified Mail growth against a declining mailstream. It is not a rounding artifact and it is not a one-year anomaly.

Why this happens

Organizations do not send Certified Mail because they want to. They send it because a statute, a regulation, a contract, or a court requires proof. That demand does not respond to email, to customer preference, or to postage increases the way discretionary mail does. When the rest of the mailstream contracts, accountable mail holds.

Five years, indexed

Indexing to fiscal 2021 shows the divergence more clearly than raw volumes, because the three series differ by orders of magnitude.

Certified Mail dipped with everything else through fiscal 2023, then turned upward while First-Class Mail and the market dominant mailstream kept falling. By fiscal 2025 the gap was roughly twelve index points against First-Class Mail.

Revenue against volume

Certified Mail revenue has grown much faster than volume, because the fee has risen repeatedly. Fiscal 2025 revenue reached $798.3 million, up 12.6 percent against a restated FY2024 base of $708.7 million, on volume growth of 2.1 percent.

Across the five years, revenue rose roughly 30 percent while volume fell about 5 percent. That gap is the price of proof, and it is the number to bring to a budget conversation. Mailers are paying substantially more for the same compliance function.

What this means for a compliance budget

If your accountable mail budget has grown while your volume has not, this is why. The answer is rarely to send less, since the requirement is external. It is usually to audit which notice types actually need which service, and to stop buying evidence you are not required to produce.

Related services

Two neighboring services moved in opposite directions in fiscal 2025, and both are worth watching.

The Return Receipt figure is the interesting one. Certified Mail volume grew while Return Receipt volume fell nearly nine percent, meaning a smaller share of Certified Mail now carries a signature service. Whether that reflects deliberate cost management, a shift in what organizations believe they must prove, or something else is a question worth answering. See the Return Receipt guide for the choice itself.

How to read this

Three cautions before anyone quotes these numbers in a board deck.

  • Pieces are not customers. Volume growth could come from more organizations mailing, or from the same organizations mailing more. RPW does not tell you which.
  • Growth is modest. Two percent is real, but it is not a boom. The significance is entirely in the contrast with a mailstream falling three to five percent a year.
  • Revenue growth is mostly price. Do not read a 12.6 percent revenue increase as demand. Volume grew 2.1 percent, and the rest is fee increases.

Method and caveats

Figures are drawn from the USPS Revenue, Pieces, and Weight summary reports, which are filed with the Postal Regulatory Commission. Certified Mail appears under Ancillary Services in the Market Dominant Products section. USPS reports in thousands; we have converted to millions and rounded to one decimal.

One methodological point matters. USPS states that prior-year figures in each report use a methodology consistent with the current year, so the same fiscal year can carry different values in different reports. Fiscal 2024 Certified Mail appears as 157.4 million pieces and $712.8 million in the FY2024 report, and as 156.5 million pieces and $708.7 million restated in the FY2025 report. Every percentage change shown above is the figure USPS published in that year's report rather than one we calculated across reports, which is why the FY2025 rows do not reconcile against the prior-year cells in the same column.

The FY2025 figures on this page were verified directly against the FY2025 RPW summary report: Certified Mail at 159,766 thousand pieces and $798,272 thousand in revenue, Return Receipts at 114,459 thousand pieces, and Registered Mail at 444 thousand pieces, with total market dominant mail down 3.2 percent and total First-Class Mail down 5.1 percent on pieces.

Fiscal years run October 1 through September 30. FY2025 ended September 30, 2025.
