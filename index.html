The data

Certified Mail is growing while the mailstream shrinks

Compliance mail does not behave like the rest of the mailstream, because nobody sends it by choice. Organizations send it because a statute, a regulation, a contract, or a court requires proof, and that demand does not respond to email or to postage increases the way discretionary mail does. Five years of USPS data show the divergence plainly.

Two services, one discipline

Start with the service you use

Certified Mail proves delivery was attempted and, with Return Receipt, who accepted it. Certificate of Mailing proves only that you mailed it. Most compliance programs need one or the other, and many need both. Each pillar page explains the service end to end and links out to the detail.

How to think about it

The chain of custody, end to end

Accountable mail is a sequence, and a break anywhere invalidates the proof everywhere. Our library is organized around these five links, because that is how the problems actually arrive.

01

Prepare

Address quality, barcode construction, label application, and the identifiers that tie a piece to a sender and a case file.

02

Submit

Electronic documentation reaches USPS before the mail does, carrying postage, service selection, and piece detail.

03

Accept

USPS acknowledges receipt and applies the round date stamp. This is the moment the legal clock starts.

04

Track

Scan events return to the mailer, recording delivery, attempted delivery, refusal, and return to sender.

05

Retain

Proof stays retrievable for as long as the governing statute, regulator, or contract requires.

About the association

Why publish all of this for free

Accountable mail is the quiet exception in a shrinking industry. While most mail classes decline, organizations keep sending Certified Mail and Certificates of Mailing because a statute, a contract, a regulator, or a judge requires it. That demand is not discretionary.

The services themselves still carry decades of manual process, and the people who operate them are usually two or three roles removed from where the rules get written. A mail supervisor should not have to reverse engineer a technical specification to find out whether a firm sheet can be filed electronically. So we write it down.

The library is open to everyone. Membership adds MTAC readouts, advance notice of changes, and a vote on the positions we carry to the Postal Service.
