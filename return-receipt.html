Return Receipt: the green card and the electronic version

Return Receipt is the service that captures a recipient signature, and it comes in two formats that cost different amounts and produce different records. Most organizations chose one of them years ago and have never revisited the decision.

A separate service

Return Receipt is not part of Certified Mail. It is an additional extra service, purchased separately and priced separately, that can be added to Certified Mail and certain other services. Certified Mail on its own gives you a mailing receipt, a tracking number, and a delivery or attempted delivery record. Return Receipt adds the recipient's signature.

Return Receipt must be purchased at the time of mailing. USPS no longer sells Return Receipt After Mailing, although a mailer whose previously purchased Return Receipt never arrived can request information from the delivery record.

Ask whether you need it at all

Certified Mail alone already establishes that USPS delivered the piece or attempted to. Return Receipt answers a narrower question: who signed. If your governing requirement is satisfied by evidence of delivery rather than by a signature, you are paying for a record you will never use. Audit this by notice type, not across the whole program.

What proof of delivery contains

Whichever format you choose, the proof of delivery record contains the same core elements:

  • The date of delivery
  • The signature of the recipient, or of the recipient's authorized agent
  • The delivery address, where it differs from the address on the mailpiece

That third element is easy to overlook and occasionally decisive. If a piece was redirected, the record shows where it actually went.

The green card

PS Form 3811, Domestic Return Receipt, is the physical card attached to the mailpiece. The recipient signs it at delivery, and USPS mails the signed card back to the sender. The output is paper with a wet signature on it.

Two details about the card surprise people:

  • It carries its own barcode and tracking number. That Related Tracking Number tracks the card itself as it travels back to you. It does not track the original mailpiece, and confusing the two is a common source of support calls.
  • A Return Receipt cannot be viewed online. The card is the record. There is no web page to pull it up on.

Electronic Return Receipt

Electronic Return Receipt captures the same signature information and returns it to the mailer electronically rather than as a physical card traveling back through the mailstream. Like the green card, it must be requested at the time of mailing.

One point of precision that matters when writing policy language: this is not an electronic PS Form 3811. PS Form 3811 is a paper card and has no electronic version. Electronic Return Receipt is a different fulfillment of the same underlying Return Receipt service. A procedure that specifies "PS Form 3811" by number is therefore specifying paper, whether or not the drafter intended to.

Eligibility is slightly narrower than the physical card. Electronic Return Receipt is not available to APO, FPO, and DPO addresses, or to certain United States territories, possessions, and freely associated states. If your mail includes those destinations, confirm coverage before converting your whole program.

Current fees

Fees below reflect USPS prices effective July 12, 2026, published in Notice 123. Certified Mail and both Return Receipt options increased on that date as part of a broader mailing services price adjustment. Always verify against Notice 123 before budgeting, and use the fee in effect on the USPS acceptance date.

Extra service fees, effective July 12, 2026
ServiceFeePrevious feeChange
Certified Mail$5.55$5.30+$0.25
Return Receipt, electronic$2.91$2.82+$0.09
Return Receipt, PS Form 3811$4.65$4.40+$0.25

The difference between the two Return Receipt formats is currently $1.74 per piece. Note that the gap widened at the July 2026 adjustment, because the green card rose by $0.25 while the electronic version rose by $0.09. An organization sending 100,000 signature-required pieces a year is looking at a $174,000 annual difference between the two formats, before any consideration of the labor involved in handling returned cards.

Cheaper is not automatically equivalent

A lower fee does not carry equal evidentiary weight in every legal or regulatory context. Confirm what your governing statute, regulation, or contract actually requires before switching formats on the basis of cost. The point of the comparison above is to make the question worth asking, not to answer it for you.

Who is allowed to sign

The signature on a Return Receipt may belong to the recipient or to the recipient's authorized agent. A Return Receipt therefore does not, by itself, establish that a named individual personally received the mailpiece. A spouse, a coworker, a receptionist, or a mailroom clerk may sign.

If your requirement is delivery to a specific named person, Return Receipt alone will not satisfy it. Restricted Delivery is the service that limits who may accept the piece, and it carries its own fee. Adult Signature services impose an age requirement on the signer. Each should be traceable to an actual requirement rather than added out of caution.

When a receipt never arrives

Green cards get lost. When a purchased PS Form 3811 does not come back, PS Form 3811-A is the mechanism for obtaining the delivery information from the USPS record instead.

The constraint that matters operationally: the request must be submitted within 90 days of the date on the mailing receipt. That is a short window relative to how long it typically takes an organization to notice a missing card, which is usually when someone goes looking for it during a dispute months later.

Build a 90-day exception report

If you use green cards at volume, run a report identifying pieces where a delivery event exists but no card has been logged, and run it inside the 90-day window. Without that report you will discover missing receipts only when it is too late to recover them, which is a structural argument for the electronic format on its own.

PS Form 3811-A is not available for items sent with Signature Confirmation Restricted Delivery, Adult Signature, or insurance for more than $500.

Deciding which one you need

Work through these in order. Most organizations discover the answer at step two or three.

  1. Find the governing language. Locate the statute, regulation, contract, or internal policy that requires a return receipt for this notice type. Read what it actually says.
  2. Check whether it names a format. Requirements written as "return receipt requested" generally describe the service, not the physical card. Requirements that specifically name PS Form 3811 are rarer than people assume.
  3. If the requirement is internal, ask who can change it. A surprising share of green card volume exists because a policy document written years ago has never been revisited, and nothing external requires it.
  4. Check your destinations. If you mail to APO, FPO, DPO, or affected territories, plan for a mixed approach rather than a clean conversion.
  5. Confirm your archive can hold the electronic record for the full retention period, retrievably, before you retire the paper process.
